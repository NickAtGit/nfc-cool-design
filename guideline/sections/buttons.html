One component parameterised by six local custom properties, so the same element re-skins by context instead of forking into a variant per surface.

Tiers

Five tiers, one component. The filled primary is the action a surface exists for: Save, Invite, Save Contact. The outlined tier is secondary, ghost is tertiary, and danger is outlined until it is the one destructive action on the surface, when it fills. Store badges stay the marketing hero's CTA; a filled button is for work, not for leaving to the store.

One blue, used as an area. The filled tier fills with #128CF0 and the outlined tier borders with the same value, so they read as one family. White on that fill is 3.48:1, under the 4.5 a 15px label needs; it is a deliberate decision, pinned in the contrast suite with its date so it stays revisitable rather than becoming an invisible regression.

Hover darkens to #0F76CA, which lifts the label to 4.70:1, so the state a person is actively pointing at is the compliant one. The test does not pin a direction, it pins the outcome: hover must never make the label harder to read. In dark mode, where the primary is brand yellow with a near-black label, that same rule makes hover brighten instead.

If it is ever revisited, #0F78CE is the nearest shade of this blue where a white label clears 4.5 at rest.

Sizes and states

One blue on a button. Blue as an area is the light stop; blue as text is the dark stop. On a button the blue is always a fill or a border and never the label, so Save and Cancel border and fill in the same #128CF0. Cancel's label is ink rather than blue: a blue label beside a blue border is two blues on one control, and the light stop is only 3.48:1 as text anyway. A border is a UI boundary, so 3:1 applies and it clears at 3.48. Ghost has no border, so its label carries the affordance and takes the dark stop, like an inline link.

Every state is defined, including :focus-visible: tab through them.

Capitals on the middle, no nudge. Block padding is even, so Titillium's capitals land within 0.25 to 0.5px of the button's middle (text snaps to whole pixels, so that is as close as it gets) and a label in lower case only sits about 1px low. Lifting every label with two pixels of bottom padding was tried in the console and read as text sitting high beside the chips and pills next to it. The guard suite fails a button padded unevenly.

Hover is for a pointer. Every hover style sits in @media (hover: hover), so a tap on an iPad does not leave a button lit; a touch gets the hover look only while the finger is down (:active). On any touch screen, (pointer: coarse), a small button takes the standard control's height, whatever the width.

In a row: quiet danger and the icon square

a destructive action beside what it acts on
36px squares on one centre line
Two volumes of danger. .btn-danger.btn-quiet is the row-level Remove and Revoke: the word without the box, in secondary ink that still clears 4.5:1, and the full danger border, label and wash the moment a pointer or the keyboard arrives. It asks first, naming the thing (the Overlay page). Full .btn-danger is kept for the action a page exists for.

An icon button is a 36px square that never shrinks. .btn-icon is the burger's and the theme icon's shape for any control whose glyph is the whole label; its name goes in aria-label. flex-shrink: 0 on every one of them, because a bar short of room squeezed the console's theme icon to 18px on a phone, which is what made it look off centre.
