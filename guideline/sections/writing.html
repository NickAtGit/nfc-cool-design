X of Y in prose, the slash only in a figure. A sentence says "12 of 20 seats used"; the slash is for the big number in a stat tile, where it is a number and not prose. A subtitle that is a sentence ends with a period, one that is a label does not.

One date style per kind of date. A day heading a group of rows is the weekday, the day and the month, "Thursday 15 October"; a moment is "15 Oct 2026, 12:45"; in the current year both drop the year. A date with no time keeps its year, because it is usually a deadline. Every date in a product comes from one helper per form, so there is no second spelling to drift.

One word per thing. A thing has one name everywhere it appears: the link to a page uses that page's title, a button's label matches the page it opens, and a concept is never two words in two places (a lead is never also a contact). When a control changes, the copy that names it changes too.

No em dashes, no dashes for nothing. In anything a person reads, a period, a colon or a comma says the same thing. A missing value is a word ("None", "Not set"), never a dash. A comment in code is not copy and keeps its dashes.

Explanations behind info tips; the rest stays visible. What a control does, a rule read once, a consequence checked while deciding: behind a mark. A subtitle, data, an empty state, a status, why something is blocked, a one-time secret warning, a destructive consequence and any link stay on the page.
